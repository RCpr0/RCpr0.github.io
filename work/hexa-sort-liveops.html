Hexa Sort · Live-ops · 2024–25

Live-ops features for a top puzzle game

My first year at Mindstorm was on Hexa Sort, a live puzzle game published by Lion Studios. I worked on its events and meta features end to end: research and flows, directing the UI and art, then the A/B tests and Looker analysis that decided what stayed and how it was tuned. (Hexa Sort isn't Hex Up!, a separate game I designed later.)

RoleGame designer, live-ops
GameHexa Sort
Years2024 – 2025
ToolsFigma · Looker · Sheets · A/B tests
How I worked

Ship it, measure it, tune it

Every feature followed the same loop. I researched how the best games in the genre handle it, wrote the spec and flows in Figma, and directed the UI and art with the art team. After launch I designed an A/B test, defined the Looker dashboards with the data team, and rebalanced based on what the numbers showed.

Lead featureCollections V2 · Roll & Win · Mystery Card

Collections, rebuilt around progress

Collections, where players complete card sets for rewards, had launched before I joined the feature. Its first version wasn't moving engagement or revenue, and the top complaint in a player survey was simple: progress felt too slow. I led the second version.

  • Diagnosis: reviewed V1's data alongside the survey results, then ranked candidate fixes on a cost-versus-impact matrix.
  • Economy: designed card-pack pricing, rarity odds and daily-deal price ramps, with simulators that projected how quickly players would complete sets before anything shipped.
  • Roll & Win: a dice event I took from ideation boards to final rules, coin-sink maths, spec and copy.
  • Mystery Card: a collection card earned outside Collections, through live events and card packs, tying the feature into the rest of the live-ops calendar.
Mystery Card

One card, two systems

The Mystery Card sits in a collection set but is earned outside it, through live events and card packs, so finishing a set pulls players into the event calendar and the event calendar feeds Collections. I designed the flow; the final screens were produced with the art team.

18% → 33%Average set progress after 12 days, V1 vs V2 day-one joiners
+95%Engagement for the winning Roll & Win free-player variant
+60%Completion for the winning Roll & Win paying-player variant
1.5 → 1.9Daily-deal transactions per engaged player after my changes

What didn't go to plan: after rewards were cut late in the cycle, set completion came in at about half my pre-launch projection. That taught me to rerun projections every time the economy changes, not just at spec time.

Chain Offer · Economy redesign

Redesigning an offer ladder players already knew

Chain Offer is a ladder of bundles where claiming one unlocks the next. The feature already existed. My job was to make it earn more without making it feel pushier.

  • Rebalanced the ladder against spender personas across four design rounds, ending on a top-heavy structure modelled on genre leaders.
  • Wrote the A/B spec, with five variants in the final round.
  • Learned from the test: opening the ladder with a paid step cut purchase rate and free-to-paid conversion, which shaped how each player segment's ladder starts.

Rainbow Launcher · Rainbow Streak

When "easier" costs money

The Rainbow Launcher is a booster that clears tiles on the board. I reran its placement A/B test to validate the first result. The best placement cut level fail rate by about a fifth, but it also cut coin spend by up to a third, because players were spending less to get through levels.

Alongside it I owned the spec for Rainbow Streak, an event that earns the launcher through win streaks. I added two new test variants, retuned the shots per streak tier and the difficulty offsets, and worked through the activation flow with the art team.

Optimisation work

Making existing features pull their weight

Weekly Quest

Finding the missions nobody finished

I specced the feature's Looker dashboard, then broke completion down by mission set. The best sets were completed many times more often than the worst, which pointed straight at the weak missions to replace. I also proposed a task-complete floater so players could see their progress mid-level.

Donut Craze

Review, art direction, polish

For this event I wrote the feature review against the genre's best version of the format, directed its art and VFX (flat sprites, shine, spin and confetti), sourced the sound and music, and ran build QA. I later did a quality-of-life pass and a rebalance of its card-pack rewards.

What I took from it

Every feature is a hypothesis

A year of live-ops taught me to design features as experiments: write down what should move before shipping, instrument it, and accept the result even when it's a trade-off, like a booster that helps players but costs coins. It's the same habit I now bring to level design.
