Why a game's first levels were losing players

A mobile studio asked why its sort puzzle was losing players in the first fifteen levels. I answered with a solver, frame-by-frame benchmarks against two genre leaders, and the studio's own funnel data. Then I re-paced the levels that needed it.

RoleIndependent consultant
ScopeLevels 1–15, FTUE
Year2026
MethodsSolver · benchmarks · funnel

Finding 1 · The rule, not the levels

Three times the choices on the same boards

The obvious guess was that the early levels were simply too hard. Measuring them said otherwise. I wrote a solver and counted the legal opening moves on every early board, both under the game's own move rule and under the rule its competitors use.

This game, own rule30.5
This game, common rule10.2
Genre leader A10.4
Genre leader B9.6

Mean legal opening moves per level.

Under a shared rule, this game's boards were actually the tamest of the three. Under its own, more permissive move rule they offered about three times as many choices. New players weren't facing harder puzzles. They were facing far more options with no signal about which ones mattered.

Finding 2 · What actually predicts failure

Scattered colours, new colours, and a broken metric

~½Of the variation in fail rate from level 5 onward is explained by colour scatter
2×Churn on levels that introduce a new colour: 15.0% vs 7.5%
L1–4Provably unlosable, yet reporting fail rates, so the funnel was mismeasured

The solver proved levels 1–4 couldn't be lost, so the "failures" reported on them were tracking something else. That one finding means the early funnel needs re-instrumenting before its numbers can be trusted.

What I changed

Re-pacing, measured

  • Solver-led re-pacing on five levels (4, 7, 8, 12 and 13), cutting colour scatter where it spiked. The producer's own hand-authored pass covered the rest.
  • Mechanic onboarding fixed: a covering mechanic introduced once and then dropped now recurs across levels 10–15, and a debut that opened too late in the level was moved to the halfway point.
  • Found two levels that are only solvable under this game's rule. One is a deliberate gate. The other is a tutorial that can't exist in either competitor, which is worth knowing before copying a competitor's pacing.

Honesty built in: the report includes a corrections log. One correlation first circulated without its caveats is restated there with its sample size, and every change that was later reverted is recorded. A study that reports only its wins misrepresents itself.
